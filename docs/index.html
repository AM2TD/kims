KIMS: Kinematics-Constrained MPPI Smoothing
for UWB-IMU 6-DoF Pose Estimation

A fixed-lag smoother that infers one compact window variable, the window-start offset and a smooth correction of the IMU input, rolls thousands of candidates through the IMU kinematics on a GPU, and carries the moments of the window posterior, not its mode, to the next window. UWB ranges and an IMU, nothing else.

◆ What KIMS does

UWB ranges are drift-free but heavy-tailed, with non-line-of-sight delays and per-link biases that persist for seconds, and anchors are usually mounted at similar heights, so near the anchor plane the ranges constrain the height only weakly. For exactly coplanar anchors the window cost has two mirror-symmetric minima, and the summary of a window that is carried into the next one, the mode and curvature of an optimizer or the single Gaussian of a filter, decides which branch the estimator follows.

KIMS recasts model predictive path integral control as a fixed-lag smoother. Its virtual control input is a 33-dimensional window variable: an offset of the window-start state (position, velocity, attitude) and a four-term cosine correction of the IMU input per channel, independent of the window length. Every candidate trajectory is generated by propagating the IMU kinematics from the window head, so it is kinematically consistent by construction. With temperature one the optimal path distribution of MPPI is the posterior of the window variable given the window's ranges, and KIMS samples it with a proposal adapted from the previous window, importance weights that correct for that proposal, and adaptive tempering whenever the weights degenerate. The estimate is the weighted mean of the rollouts, the next proposal is the weighted moments moved by one epoch, and the arrival prior of the new window head is one Kalman step with the measurements of the leaving epoch only, so the carried covariance and the window cost never share a measurement.

One solve: UWB ranges and IMU inputs are accumulated (a), N window variables are drawn from the proposal (b), each is propagated through the IMU kinematics from the window head (c), and the UWB residuals with the prior-to-proposal ratio give importance weights whose moments form the estimate and the next proposal (d).

One window shift. The leaving epoch updates the arrival prior of the new head (red); the weighted moments of this solve, moved by one epoch, become the next proposal (blue).

Kinematics-constrained

Every rollout is an IMU integration with a smooth input correction. The sampled dimension stays at 33 for a window of 100–230 UWB epochs.

Moments, not modes

The weighted mean and sample covariance keep both branches of a posterior that splits near a coplanar anchor plane. A matched Levenberg–Marquardt solver of the same objective carries the mode and its curvature and drops a branch in 30 % of such windows.

Divergence, large errors and mirror-branch tracking

A run counts as diverged when its RMSE exceeds 100 m, far above the 3.2 m of the worst non-diverged run. The practical failure threshold is 1 m. Mirror-branch tracking, an estimate nearer the mirrored than the true ground truth while the vehicle is more than 0.25 m from the anchor plane, is reported apart because its error can stay below 1 m: KIMS follows the mirror branch on eee_03 and nya_02 for 34 and 74 % of the scored time (its two runs above 1 m), on nya_01 and nya_03 for 21–23 %, and on three MILUV flights for 2–8 %.

Sampling versus the matched LM solver

To separate the inference step from the parameterization, the matched LM solver keeps the window variable, the prior, the cost, the GPU rollouts, the proposal and the window shift of KIMS and replaces only the weighted samples: it minimizes the same objective from the proposal mean (at most eight iterations, gradient and Hessian from central finite differences over 1,123 of the same rollouts) and draws its samples from the Laplace approximation. Over the 104 sequences it is never more accurate than KIMS by more than 1 cm and worse on 14, and on the coplanar IULUI flights it stays on the mirror branch for 13 and 10 % of the flight (KIMS 0.0 and 0.1 %), which accounts for the whole gap (0.548 against 0.120 m, 0.418 against 0.118 m). With the same rollouts it takes 12.7 ms per MILUV epoch against 9.0 ms for KIMS.

🛰 The IULUI dataset

The arena: an OmniNxt aerial robot with two Nooploop LinkTrack P-B UWB tags, an IMU and a Jetson Orin NX, four LinkTrack anchors on tripods, and ten Qualisys Arqus cameras for ground truth.

The repository ships IULUI, our UWB-IMU flight campaign. Two tags range to four anchors at 800 ranges per second, the IMU runs at 50 Hz and Qualisys ground truth at 70 Hz; raw ranges, IMU and ground truth are released as CSV with the anchor and tag geometry in JSON. Desktop. The mean solve time is below the epoch interval on every dataset. On MILUV the tempered solves form a tail that arrives in bursts, and a first-in-first-out replay delays some estimates by up to 2.1 s.

Jetson Orin NX. The same runs reproduce the desktop trajectories within 3 cm, but one sampling pass takes 24–39 ms, five to seven times the desktop, so at N = 8,192 the median solve exceeds the epoch interval on every dataset by 1.3–3.5 times. A smaller N does not close the gap: on CoplanarSquareTurn the Jetson time is lowest at N = 2,048 with a median of 10.9 ms against the 10 ms interval, on MILUV and UTIL N = 2,048 is slower than 8,192 through extra tempering, and only NTU VIRAL keeps up at N = 2,048 (7.8 against 17 ms). KIMS therefore sustains the sensor rate on the desktop GPU, and we claim no onboard real-time operation at the final settings.

Median solve time per epoch on the Jetson Orin NX against the rollout count on CoplanarSquareTurn.

Two latencies. The times above are the cost of producing one solve. Separate from it is the estimation latency of fixed-lag smoothing: the committed state is the oldest one in the window, so it trails the newest measurement by 2–3 s. The newest state of the window is available at every solve as a lag-0 output, at the RMSE in the last column.

⚖ Comparison conditions

SFUISE runs with its authors' published configuration (10 Hz spline knots, 10 s sliding window, 0.3 m residual gate), receives the unbinned ranges with the lever arm of every tag (the TDOA stream of the single tag on UTIL) and the IMU as configured in its release, estimates its own IMU biases, and its spline is evaluated at the body origin before scoring. The scored output is its last estimate of each state before that state leaves the window, about 10 s after its time, which does not explain its accuracy: read 0, 2 or 10 s after the measurement on eight UTIL flights it gives 0.139, 0.127 and 0.125 m against 0.135 and 0.130 m for KIMS at lag 0 and 2 s.

Short-window test. In our configuration, with only its window shortened to that of KIMS (3 s on MILUV, 2 s elsewhere) and every other setting unchanged, SFUISE diverged on all 33 sequences we ran. With its residual gate widened from 0.3 to 1.5 m it tracked the three MILUV flights we retried at 3–13 times its 10 s error (0.868, 1.462 and 1.101 m against 0.123, 0.114 and 0.368 m) while the three NTU VIRAL sequences still diverged, so in our setting the short window degrades SFUISE and the published gate turns the degradation into divergence. Dataset filters. The MILUV EKF additionally uses a laser height sensor and starts from the ground-truth state; the UTIL ESKF is the development-kit filter. Both are quoted as published, and our runs of the released code, used for the per-epoch statistics, match the published RMSE within 1 cm on MILUV and are within 5 mm or lower on UTIL (median 0.217 against 0.220 m).

Common protocol. All methods share the same sensor streams and extrinsics and start from the same pose: the tilt from gravity alignment at rest, zero yaw except on NTU VIRAL (rest-phase estimate), and the take-off position from the anchor-frame origin (MILUV), the first ground-truth sample (IULUI, NTU VIRAL eee), the development-kit start (UTIL) or a rest-phase range fit below the anchor plane (NTU VIRAL nya, sbs). The 3D RMSE of the committed output is scored over the whole ground-truth span without alignment, except on NTU VIRAL, whose Leica frame is not surveyed in the anchor frame: there the prism point is aligned by a yaw-and-translation fit and scored 10 s from both ends. The earlier in-repository baselines (EKF-RTS, UKF-RTS, sliding-window pose-graph optimization, particle smoothing) remain available through scripts/run.py --method.

⚡ Quick start

# build (offline, no ROS needed)
cmake -S . -B build -DKIMS_NO_ROS=ON && cmake --build build -j8

# data: IULUI ships in data/iului; MILUV, NTU VIRAL and UTIL are fetched or converted
scripts/prepare_data.sh miluv
scripts/prepare_data.sh ntu <dir with the NTU VIRAL zips>
scripts/prepare_data.sh util <UTIL root>

# KIMS on all 104 sequences, then the tables
scripts/reproduce.sh
scripts/reproduce.sh miluv tables      # one dataset only

Per-dataset instructions and the settings of every run are in the README.
