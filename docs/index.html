KIMS

Kinematics-Constrained MPPI Smoothing for UWB-IMU 6-DoF Pose Estimation

Code Paper (under review) Video Results

Abstract

This paper presents KIMS, a kinematics-constrained fixed-lag smoother for 6-DoF pose estimation from ultra-wideband (UWB) ranges and an inertial measurement unit (IMU). UWB ranges contain non-line-of-sight outliers and per-link biases, and anchors mounted at similar heights constrain the height only weakly, so the posterior of a window is non-Gaussian. An estimator that carries a single estimate with its curvature describes it by one local fit. KIMS instead samples a compact window variable, the window-start offset and a smooth correction of the IMU input. Every sample is rolled through the IMU kinematics and weighted by the robust range cost of the whole window, as in model predictive path integral control, and the estimate and the proposal carried to the next window are moments of the window posterior rather than its mode. On 104 real flights from four datasets KIMS never diverges and exceeds 1 m of error on two, near coplanar anchors it is more robust than a gradient-based optimizer of the same objective, and on an embedded GPU its median solve time is below the ranging interval.

Pipeline

Sample33 numbers per sample: a start-state offset and 4 cosine terms per IMU channel
Roll outevery sample is an IMU trajectory, so every hypothesis is kinematically feasible
Weight and carryUWB likelihood weights, tempered when they collapse, and the window moves on by its moments

Tempering

Samples are drawn from the proposal carried from the previous window and weighted against the window posterior. When the weights collapse, the cost is tempered by \(\beta \in (0,1]\) and raised back to 1 over a few redraws.

\[ q = \alpha\,\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}) + (1-\alpha)\,p_0,\ \ \alpha = 0.9,\qquad w^{(n)}(\beta) \propto e^{-\beta S(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)})}\,\frac{p_0(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)})}{q(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)})},\qquad \mathrm{ESS}(\beta) = \frac{1}{\sum_n w^{(n)}(\beta)^2} \]
  1. Tempering starts when \(\mathrm{ESS}(\beta{=}1) < 0.02N\).
  2. Bisection on the samples at hand, without new rollouts, sets the first \(\beta\) to the largest value with \(\mathrm{ESS}(\beta) \ge N_{\mathrm{fit}} = 4\dim\boldsymbol{\theta} = 132\).
  3. The Gaussian component is refitted to the weights \(w(\beta)\) as \(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}, \tilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\) and blended with the covariance it was drawn from, \[ \boldsymbol{\Sigma} \leftarrow \rho\cdot 1.2\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}} + (1-\rho)\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma},\qquad \rho = \frac{\mathrm{ESS}}{\mathrm{ESS} + \dim\boldsymbol{\theta}}, \] then \(N\) new samples are drawn and rolled out.
  4. With the target \(N_\star = \max\bigl(\mathrm{ESS}(\beta)/2,\ N_{\mathrm{fit}}\bigr)\) on the new samples, \[ \beta \leftarrow \begin{cases} 1, & \mathrm{ESS}(\beta{=}1) \ge N_\star \\ \max\{\beta' \ge \beta : \mathrm{ESS}(\beta') \ge N_\star\}, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} \]
  5. Steps 3 and 4 repeat until \(\beta = 1\) and \(\mathrm{ESS}(\beta{=}1) \ge N_{\mathrm{done}} = 0.05N\), or for at most 20 stages.
  6. The estimate is the mean under the untempered weights \(w(1)\). \(\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\) is carried to the next window only if \(\mathrm{ESS}(\beta{=}1) \ge N_{\mathrm{fit}}\), otherwise the next window draws from \(p_0\).

\(\nu\) is the mean range rate of the flights. \(\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is the range standard deviation inside the cut of the loss, on MILUV the line-of-sight width of the mixture divided by the square root of its line-of-sight share, \(0.069/\sqrt{0.733}\), on NTU VIRAL the standard deviation the dataset reports with each range, on UTIL the TDoA deviation of the development kit and on IULUI the range deviation of the loss. \(\kappa = (h/r)^2\) at a typical height \(h\) above the anchor plane and anchor distance \(r\), 0.16 m at 4.0 m on MILUV, 2.2 m at 25 m on NTU VIRAL and 0.4 m at 4.4 m on IULUI. UTIL has no common anchor plane, so \(\kappa\) is the mean squared vertical direction cosine along the flights.

Split posteriors near the anchor plane

The same window objective solved by Levenberg–Marquardt is never better than KIMS by more than 1 cm, and worse on 15 of 104 flights. All 15 pass close to the anchor plane, where the posterior splits in two and a mode follows one branch. Inflating the carried covariance four times changes nothing. Restarting LM from eight points frees it on three of the four flights shown but not on CoplanarCircleSpin, where the mirrored branch holds the lower window objective from every start, while the weighted mean of KIMS follows the mass of the branches.

Solve time

Onboard Jetson Orin NX: 8.0 ms median at N = 2048, the paper setting, inside the 10 ms epoch of IULUI.

Quick start

# build (CUDA GPU, no ROS)
cmake -S . -B build -DKIMS_NO_ROS=ON && cmake --build build -j8

# one flight, score, plot
python3 scripts/run.py kims iului SquareTurn
python3 scripts/evaluate.py
python3 scripts/plot.py iului SquareTurn

# all 104 flights (or: docker/build.sh && docker/run.sh)
scripts/reproduce.sh

# online, ROS Noetic: catkin_make, then a shipped bag in real time
roslaunch kims kims_online.launch seq:=SquareTurn rviz:=true
