KIMS: Kinematics-Constrained MPPI Smoothing
for UWB-IMU 6-DoF Pose Estimation

A fixed-lag smoother for UWB-IMU pose estimation that solves each window by sampling. Thousands of candidate trajectories are rolled through the IMU kinematics on a GPU and weighted by the range residuals, and the window posterior is carried forward by its moments. It needs UWB ranges, an IMU and the anchor positions.

◆ What KIMS does

UWB ranges do not drift, but two properties of UWB-IMU data make the posterior of a sliding window non-Gaussian. Range errors are heavy-tailed, with non-line-of-sight delays and per-link biases that persist for seconds, and anchors are usually mounted at similar heights, so near the anchor plane the ranges constrain the height above it only weakly. With exactly coplanar anchors the posterior can even split into two mirror-symmetric branches, the flip ambiguity of range-based localization. An estimator that carries each window into the next as a mode with its curvature describes such a posterior by one local fit, and near the plane by only the branch its iteration reached.

KIMS treats one window as a sampling problem in the form of model predictive path integral control. The sampled window variable has 33 dimensions whatever the window length: an offset of the window-start state (position, velocity, attitude) and four cosine coefficients per IMU channel that correct the measured input. Each sample is integrated through the IMU kinematics from the window head, so every candidate is a trajectory the IMU could have produced. With temperature one the optimal path distribution of MPPI is the posterior of this variable given the window's ranges. KIMS draws from a proposal carried over from the previous window, corrects for it in the importance weights, and tempers the range cost when the weights collapse. The estimate is the weighted mean of the rollouts. The weighted moments, moved by one epoch, become the next proposal, and the arrival prior of the new head is one Kalman step with the leaving epoch's measurements only, so no measurement is counted twice.

One solve over a window of T UWB epochs. (a) Ranges, the IMU input of every inter-epoch interval and the arrival prior of the head. (b) N window variables drawn from the proposal, each a start-state offset and a four-term cosine input correction. (c) Each rollout starts from the head offset by its sample and integrates its corrected input through the IMU kinematics. (d) Range cost and prior-to-proposal ratio give the importance weights, tempered when the effective sample size is low. Their moments form the estimate and the next proposal. Panels (b) to (d) show one CoplanarSquareTurn solve in mid-flight with every eighth rollout, colored by weight in (d).

Kinematics-constrained

Every rollout is an IMU integration with a smooth input correction. The sampled dimension is 33 whether the window holds 100 or 230 UWB epochs.

Moments, not modes

The weighted mean and covariance carry a posterior that splits near a coplanar anchor plane with the spread between its branches. A Levenberg–Marquardt solver of the same objective carries the mode and its curvature instead and, after a plane crossing, stays on the mirror branch for 10 to 13 % of the coplanar IULUI flights.

Baselines with the same loss

To find where the robustness of KIMS comes from, three estimators take its Tukey loss together with its window, range standard deviation, IMU noise densities, bias prior and start state, and differ from it only in enforcing the IMU kinematics as a penalty or in sampling the states instead of the window variable. SW-PGO is a sliding-window pose-graph optimization with a node per ranging epoch, the kinematics as penalties and one bias per window, solved by Ceres. SFUISE replaces its gate by the Tukey weight, and the bootstrap particle filter (PF) propagates 8,192 particles through the measured IMU, once with the process noise of the densities and once with ten times that noise.

UTIL here is the first trial of each constellation in both TDoA modes, eight flights. On UTIL, where no flight nears a mirror configuration, every estimator except the PF with the process noise of the densities is within 1 cm of KIMS, so there the robust loss alone suffices. On MILUV SW-PGO with the Tukey loss has the lowest median after KIMS but diverges on two flights. On NTU VIRAL it has the lowest median of all, stays off the mirror branch on nya_02, which KIMS and LM both follow, and fails on sbs_02 and nya_03, where KIMS does not. Without the robust loss its medians rise on every dataset but UTIL, but it no longer diverges, since a quadratic loss keeps pulling at any distance. SFUISE with the Tukey weight no longer diverges but fails on 10 of the 13 MILUV flights and on every NTU VIRAL flight, because the ranges that the gate rejected now bend the spline from inside the cut. Two conclusions follow. The window variable by itself does not raise the accuracy, since LM, which optimizes it with the same loss, is not more accurate than SW-PGO in median on any dataset, so the margin of KIMS over both comes from the sampling and the carried moments. The window variable with its prior does keep the estimate on a path the IMU can explain, since neither KIMS nor LM ever diverges, whereas an estimator over the states can drift further than the cut from the ranges, where the bounded loss gives no pull and nothing brings it back, as the particle filter does on every flight.

Why sampling: moments instead of modes

The matched LM solver keeps everything else of KIMS, the window variable, prior, cost, GPU rollouts, proposal and window shift, and replaces the weighted samples by the mode of the same objective and its Laplace approximation. The two agree while the window posterior is a single Gaussian branch and differ when it splits near a coplanar anchor plane. The sample moments rank the branches by their mass and keep the spread between them, whereas the Laplace approximation describes only the branch the iteration reached, and since the carried summary becomes the next proposal, a recursion that has carried the lighter branch returns only when later measurements outweigh the carried curvature. Over the 104 sequences LM is never more accurate than KIMS by more than 1 cm and is less accurate by more than 1 cm on 14. Most of the gap sits on the four flights below, where LM is worse by 0.30 to 0.52 m, whereas on NTU VIRAL both solvers follow the mirror branch on the same sequences for about the same time. Solving the same windows from a common fixed prior isolates the cause. The head states of the two solvers then agree within 5.5 mm far from the plane and within 3 mm in median near it, where LM even reaches the lower objective, so the difference lies in what is carried to the next window.

🛰 The IULUI dataset

The arena: an OmniNxt aerial robot with two Nooploop LinkTrack P-B UWB tags, an IMU and a Jetson Orin NX, four LinkTrack anchors on tripods, and ten Qualisys Arqus cameras for ground truth.

The repository ships IULUI, our own UWB-IMU flight campaign. Two tags range to four anchors at 800 ranges per second, the IMU runs at 50 Hz and Qualisys ground truth at 70 Hz. ⏱ Computation time and latency

Solve time per epoch is measured on IULUI, whose 10 ms epoch interval is the shortest of the four datasets, one process at a time on a Jetson Orin NX (MAXN, clocks fixed) with FP32 rollouts and eight host threads.

Solve time per epoch on the Jetson Orin NX against the rollout count on CoplanarSquareTurn: boxes the quartiles, whiskers 1.5 interquartile ranges, dotted line the 10 ms epoch interval, right axis the RMSE of the same runs.

Rollout count. At N = 8,192 the median solve takes 22.8 ms. The RMSE is flat from N = 1,024 upward. Below that the first pass collapses on part of the solves and the tempering passes make the solve slower, not faster. From N = 1,280 to 2,560 the median stays under the epoch interval, 6.0 to 9.6 ms, at the same RMSE as N = 8,192. Real-time operation on the Jetson therefore means staying in that range of N, or shortening the window, since the window length sets the cost of every rollout.

Two latencies. The time above is what one solve costs. The estimation latency of fixed-lag smoothing is a separate matter: the committed state is the oldest one in the window, so it trails the newest measurement by the window length of 2 to 3 s. The newest state of the window is also available at every solve as a lag-0 output.

⚖ Comparison conditions

SFUISE runs with its authors' published configuration (10 Hz spline knots, 10 s sliding window, 0.3 m residual gate). It receives the unbinned ranges with the lever arm of every tag (the TDOA stream of the single tag on UTIL) and the IMU as configured in its release, estimates its own IMU biases, and its spline is evaluated at the body origin before scoring. The scored output is its last estimate of each state before that state leaves the window, about 10 s after its time. That lag does not explain its accuracy: read 0, 2 or 10 s after the measurement on eight UTIL flights it gives 0.139, 0.127 and 0.125 m, against 0.135 and 0.130 m for KIMS at lag 0 and 2 s.

Short-window test. With only its window shortened to that of KIMS (3 s on MILUV, 2 s elsewhere) and every other setting unchanged, SFUISE diverged on all 33 sequences we ran. With the residual gate widened from 0.3 to 1.5 m it tracked the three MILUV flights we retried at 3 to 13 times its 10 s error (0.868, 1.462 and 1.101 m against 0.123, 0.114 and 0.368 m), while the three NTU VIRAL sequences still diverged. In our setting, then, the short window degrades SFUISE and the published gate turns that degradation into divergence. The per-sequence results:

Dataset filters. The MILUV EKF additionally uses a laser height sensor and starts from the ground-truth state; the UTIL ESKF is the development-kit filter. Both are our runs of the released code, which reproduce the published RMSE within 9 mm on MILUV and within 1 cm on most UTIL flights (median 0.217 against the published 0.220 m).

Common protocol. All methods see the same sensor streams and extrinsics and start from the same pose: tilt from gravity alignment at rest, zero yaw except on NTU VIRAL (rest-phase estimate), and the take-off position from the anchor-frame origin (MILUV), the first ground-truth sample (IULUI, NTU VIRAL eee), the development-kit start (UTIL) or a rest-phase range fit (NTU VIRAL nya, sbs). The 3D RMSE of the committed output is scored over the whole ground-truth span without alignment, except on NTU VIRAL, whose Leica frame is not surveyed in the anchor frame. There the prism point is aligned by a yaw-and-translation fit and scored 10 s from both ends. The earlier in-repository baselines (EKF-RTS, UKF-RTS, sliding-window pose-graph optimization, particle smoothing) are still available through scripts/run.py --method.

⚡ Quick start

# build (offline, no ROS needed)
cmake -S . -B build -DKIMS_NO_ROS=ON && cmake --build build -j8

# data: IULUI ships in data/iului; MILUV, NTU VIRAL and UTIL are fetched or converted
scripts/prepare_data.sh miluv
scripts/prepare_data.sh ntu <dir with the NTU VIRAL zips>
scripts/prepare_data.sh util <UTIL root>

# KIMS on all 104 sequences, then the tables
scripts/reproduce.sh
scripts/reproduce.sh miluv tables      # one dataset only
